5Data pipeline — from raw story audio to a training set

The dataset is built entirely on a laptop CPU; only training needs a GPU. Each step exists to protect the one thing that matters most for a fine-tune: the transcript matches the audio, and the audio is clean.

  1. Acquisition. Marathi/Hindi story audio is downloaded from YouTube / YT-Music and marathi-stories.com (via the story-audio-fetcher workflow). Background music is stripped with demucs at this stage. Why: the model must learn a clean voice, not a backing track — any residual music becomes baked-in noise.
  2. Denoise. scripts/phase1_dataprep/denoise_chunks.py runs the noisereduce spectral-gating denoiser (prop_decrease=0.5, stationary=True, RMS loudness restored), with an optional ffmpeg atempo slowdown (0.90 = 10% slower, used on Maya). Why: spectral gating removes steady hiss; restoring RMS keeps loudness consistent so the model doesn't learn volume swings.
  3. Segment. segment_audio.py uses pydub silence splitting (−40 dBFS, min-silence 500 ms, keep 250 ms) into 2–20 s chunks at 44.1 kHz mono. Why: Parler trains on short utterances; natural silence cuts avoid clipping words mid-syllable.
  4. Transcribe. transcribe.py uses faster-whisper large-v3 int8 (lang=mr), or transcribe_saaras.py uses Sarvam saaras:v3 codemix (mr-IN, keeps English in Latin script). Why: a code-mix-aware transcriber preserves the English words Marathi narrators actually use, instead of mangling them into Devanagari.
  5. Human review — the #1 quality lever. 3 days of listening: fix numbers → words, proper names, code-mix, and add commas for prosody; remove any clip whose transcript doesn't match. Why: mismatched transcript↔audio pairs are the top cause of a bad fine-tune — the model learns to "say" the wrong tokens.
  6. Disfluency flagging. flag_disfluent.py flags low chars/sec clips (self-corrections, stutters). Why: narrators restart sentences; those clips teach the model to stammer.
  7. Build. build_multispeaker_dataset.py writes a parquet with embedded audio; each speaker gets a per-speaker 2% test split, then all are concatenated (so the test set covers every voice and eval/loss is meaningful). Caption template: "{name} narrates a children's story in an expressive, warm, animated storytelling tone with emotional variation, at a {pace} pace. Very clear audio with no background noise." Sunita's train rows are oversampled ×2 to anchor the low-resource target voice. Why: identity is the caption name (§6), so a clean per-name split and oversampling the scarce target are what make the voice stick.
Why this now motivates synthetic data Every clip in steps 1–2 comes from a scraped recording. That carries copyright and voice-likeness exposure (§12). The cleaner long-term path is to generate the narration audio ourselves — which is exactly the next-step Gemini synthetic-data plan.

6How the fine-tuning actually works

VERDICT. Parler-TTS treats speech as a language-modeling problem over learned audio tokens. An autoregressive decoder LM predicts discrete DAC (Descript Audio Codec) tokens, conditioned by cross-attention on a frozen Flan-T5 encoding of the English description, plus the transcript. The objective is plain cross-entropy over audio tokens — no spectrograms, no GAN.
Crucial correction Parler does NOT train on mel-spectrograms and uses NO GAN / mel loss. Those belong to VITS / HiFi-GAN. Any mel and adversarial losses live inside the separately-trained DAC codec — never in the Parler LM we fine-tune. If you ever see someone watching loss_mel or loss_disc on this project, it's the wrong mental model.

6.1 Identity: a name welded into the weights

The speaker is simply a name token in the caption (e.g. "Divya"). Fine-tuning binds that name to a learned timbre inside the weights — a "welded" identity, not a reference clip you pass at inference. Two concepts to separate:

  • Timbre = the per-name acoustic signature stored in the weights. This is what "Sunita sounds like Sunita" means.
  • Prosody / pace / emotion = driven by the caption's style words ("expressive, warm, animated… at a moderate pace") plus the transcript's punctuation (commas = pauses).

This is precisely why putting three different voices under one "Sunita" name in v2/v3 muddied the timbre — the weights tried to weld three signatures to one token. And it's why naming them separately and oversampling the target fixed it in v6.

6.2 What happens at the start of training

  • Flan-T5 stays frozen → the conditioning representation is fixed; we only learn how to use it.
  • The decoder starts random with respect to the new name→timbre and caption→style mappings; warmup + a low learning rate stabilize the first steps, which are noisy.
  • The name binding sharpens over the first epoch — this is the audible moment the voice "locks in".
  • The base model already heard ~45,000 hours, so we are only re-pointing a name at a timbre, not learning speech from scratch → a few hours per speaker, ~2–4 epochs, and we stop when eval/loss rises (overfitting).

Refs: Parler / Stable-TTS arXiv:2402.01912 · DAC codec arXiv:2306.06546 · huggingface/parler-tts · ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts (69 voices / 21 languages).

7The failed run — the disk-space math

One run, toasty-fog-25 (id vz6mq40v), died with "No space left on device" at exactly step 600. That is 2 × save_steps(300) — not a coincidence.

Parler's training loop is a manual accelerate loop, not the HF Trainer. It honors the integer save_steps (and ignores save_strategy), and rotate_checkpoints writes the new checkpoint before deleting the old one. So at the second save, two full checkpoints coexist briefly.

Worked calculation — why it crashed at step 600
Component (one full save_state checkpoint, ~0.9B model)Size
Model weights (bf16, 2 bytes/param)1.8 GB
AdamW optimizer states (fp32 m + v, 8 bytes/param)7.2 GB
fp32 master copy of weights3.6 GB
= one checkpoint~12 GB
At the 2nd save, checkpoint-300 + checkpoint-600 coexist → transient~24 GB
+ dataset (~5 GB) + token cache (~3 GB) + base model (~6 GB)~14 GB
Peak on a 40–50 GB volume~38 GB → overflow
The ~24 GB transient checkpoint spike, stacked on the ~14 GB of fixed data, blew past the small volume → crash at step 600.
The fix (used on every run since) Set save_steps: 100000 (far larger than the ~1000 total steps) so no intermediate checkpoints are ever written; the final model is still saved at the end via save_pretrained. Trade-off: no mid-run resume if the pod dies — but a Marathi fine-tune is only ~1–2 hours, so we simply restart. This single line is why v5 and v6 ran clean to completion.

8Evaluation framework

TTS quality has no single number. We track a panel of objective (machine), subjective (human), and system metrics. The stars are how much each matters for an expressive storytelling product.

8.1 Metric-importance table

MetricTypeImportanceWhat it measures · how
WERObjective★★★★★Word-error-rate: is the speech intelligible? Transcribe output, compare to target text. Reference-free (needs an ASR model).
CERObjective★★★★Character-error-rate — finer-grained than WER, better for Indic scripts. Reference-free (ASR).
UTMOSObjective★★★★A neural network's predicted MOS — naturalness without humans. Reference-free.
Speaker SimilarityObjective★★★★Does it sound like the target speaker? Cosine of ECAPA-TDNN embeddings vs a reference clip. Reference-needed.
MOSSubjective★★★★★Mean Opinion Score — humans rate naturalness 1–5. Human.
CMOSSubjective★★★★★Comparative MOS — humans pick A vs B (e.g. v6 vs Gemini) on a −3…+3 scale. Human.
Prosody EvaluationSubjective★★★★★Is the storytelling rhythm/emotion right? Expert listening. Human.
Long-form StabilitySubjective★★★★Does it stay coherent over a multi-minute story (no drift/artifacts)? Human.
RTFSystem★★★Real-time factor: seconds of compute per second of audio. Machine.

8.3 How we will populate it

  • scripts/narrate/compare_squim.py computes UTMOS + SQUIM PESQ / STOI / SI-SDR (all reference-free) and optional WER via Sarvam.
  • MOS / CMOS need human raters on a fixed story set.
  • Speaker-Sim = ECAPA-TDNN cosine vs a Sunita reference clip.
  • CER = character-level error, computed alongside WER.
  • The base-vs-v6 A/B on the two stories (§4.1) is currently done by listening (v6 better, esp. Hindi); the objective numbers are the next step.
Read WER relatively, not absolutely Whisper is English-centric, so absolute WER for Marathi is unreliable — the whole project sits near ~100%. What matters is the relative movement: v6 breaking below 100% (96.2) for the first time is the meaningful signal, not the absolute value.

9.2 Why not zero-shot cloning? The IndicF5 trial

scripts/narrate/narrate_indicf5.py runs ai4bharat/IndicF5 zero-shot reference-clip cloning (24 kHz). The founder tried it on RunPod with one reference wav → outputs were unsatisfactory: cloning trades away the learned prosody/pronunciation you get from a fine-tune. This is exactly why we chose the fine-tuning path over zero-shot cloning.

10.2 Minimum GPU — and the VRAM math

Minimum GPU 24 GB Ampere/Ada (A5000 / L4 / RTX 4090). NOT 16 GB. Avoid T4/V100 — no native bf16.
Worked VRAM calculation (batch 4)
ComponentVRAM
bf16 weights1.8 GB
gradients (bf16)1.8 GB
AdamW fp32 states (m + v)7.2 GB
fp32 master weights3.6 GB
= model + optimizer14.4 GB
frozen Flan-T5-large~1.5 GB
activations + DAC + CUDA + autoregressive eval-gen KV-cacheseveral GB

A 16 GB card OOMs specifically during periodic eval-generation (the KV-cache for autoregressive sampling is the spike); 24 GB fits comfortably. AdamW = 16 bytes/param in mixed precision.

11Deployment & cost

The fine-tuned model is ≈3.5 GB safetensors (3.75 GB on HF including tokenizer) — small enough to load in seconds.

11.1 Serving

  • RunPod Serverless: a container + 3.5 GB weights load in seconds. Mitigate cold start with min-warm / active workers, flashboot, or network-volume-cached weights. Per-second billing; flex scales to zero.

12Recommendations & next steps

  1. Ship/advance v6 as the current best — sub-100 WER, best CLAP, strong Hindi. Keep Usha / Sunita and Divya; retire Maya (harsh, and its dominance hurt balance).
  2. Compute the objective benchmark panel (compare_squim.py on a GPU) to fill §8.2, then add a human MOS / CMOS A/B vs Gemini on a fixed story set.
  3. ⚙ NEXT GOAL (founder directive, 2026-06-30): build a copyright-safe dataset with Gemini. Generate animated/kids story scripts, synthesize narration audio via the Gemini TTS API, and fine-tune Parler on that synthetic data — removing the source-recording copyright and voice-likeness exposure of YouTube/website audio.
Legal caveat to state plainly Scraped audio carries two risks: (1) source-recording copyright, and (2) right-of-publicity / voice-likeness. "A voice isn't copyrightable" is not a safe defense — see the ELVIS Act (2024). But before relying on Gemini-distillation commercially, check Google's Gemini API terms (no developing competing models; paid vs unpaid data-use differ).
