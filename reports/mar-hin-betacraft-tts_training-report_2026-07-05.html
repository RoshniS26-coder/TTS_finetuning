Appendix — How to read these graphs (plain language)

Parler-TTS turns text into speech by predicting audio as a sequence of discrete tokens — like a language model predicting the next word, except each "word" is a small chunk of sound. Training teaches it to predict the right sound-tokens for given text; the loss measures how wrong those predictions are. There is no mel-spectrogram or GAN loss here — the only objective is cross-entropy over audio tokens.

What we optimise — and our "mAP"

In a YOLOv8 detection project you optimise a single north-star metric, mAP. TTS breaks that pattern: we minimise a token-prediction loss during training, but a lower loss does not reliably mean better-sounding speech. So loss is the training signal, not the quality verdict. The closest analog to mAP here is WER (Word Error Rate) from a reliable Devanagari STT (Sarvam), alongside CLAP, UTMOS, SQUIM, speaker-cosine and a human MOS panel.

⚠️ The eval/wer and percent_clean_samples logged inside W&B use an English Whisper model, which mis-scores correct Marathi/Hindi. Read them as a rough trend only — the trustworthy WER/CER comes from Sarvam Saaras run offline (next step), plus a human listen.
